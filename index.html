Cambridge Test 2 Task 1 • Official Candidate Script (3.png)
Awarded Band 6.0

Candidate's Submission:

The maps display how the café changes after it was redeveloped. It compares how as it is now and as it is before redesign. First of all before the modifications, we can see that there was a kitchen and a serving area at the top left hand corner of the café. Then, in the midle, there was the seatting area, and just above, two staff dining rooms. After that, just at the bottom of the entrance, there was bin and there were the toilets. However we can see that now, the bins have been changed into recycling bins. In addition, the staff dining rooms have been replaced with a take away food and a coffee bar. Although the seating area hasn't been modificated, on the left side, a salad bar has been added. At the top left-hand corner, the kitchen remains the same, while the serving area has been transformed into hot meals. Finally, a outdoor seating area and a barbecue have been constructed at the bottom of the café.

Official Examiner's Commentary:

Task Achievement: This response addresses all of the changes illustrated in the college café plans. The writer describes how the café looked before the modifications, then moves on to the present day map. There is no overview, or summary of the information, but all main changes have been captured.

Coherence and Cohesion: Information is generally arranged coherently. The first paragraph is an introduction, and the second contains the rest of the comparison. There is evidence of linking expressions [First of all | Then | After that | Although], however, there is a lack of reference, resulting in repetition [as it is].

Lexical Resource: Vocabulary is generally good [modifications | replaced with | transformed into] but errors remain [redesign / redesign | midle / middle | seatting / seating | modificated / modified].

Grammatical Range and Accuracy: Grammatical range is mixed. There are examples of complex sentence forms, but there is frequent repetition of verb forms e.g passive structures [have been changed | have been replaced | hasn't been modificated | has been added] which means the range is limited and precludes a higher score.

Examiner's Recommendation for Band 7+:

To improve the score, an overview that summarises the main changes should be added. It would also be beneficial to show greater accuracy in vocabulary and a wider range of grammatical structures.

Cambridge Example Overview:
"We can see that the overall shape of the college café has remained the same, with some additions, for example, outdoor areas have been added, the staff areas have been removed and the centralised serving area is now distributed to various locations in the café."
Model Exemplar • Task 1 Target Band 9.0

Examiner Band 9 Model Essay:

The two floor plans illustrate the architectural alterations made to a college café during its renovation, comparing the previous layout with its current configuration. Overall, the café has undergone a substantial redevelopment. While the basic perimeter and essential services remain unchanged, the interior has been reconfigured from a single centralized food service into multiple specialized stations, accompanied by the removal of private staff rooms and the introduction of extensive outdoor seating. Prior to redesign, the café featured a single serving counter catering to all food and drink situated directly adjacent to the kitchen in the north-western corner. In the central northern section, two dedicated staff dining rooms were partitioned off. Seating comprising tables and chairs occupied the main communal hall, while an individual waste bin and customer toilets were positioned along the eastern and south-eastern boundaries respectively. In the current layout, the kitchen retains its original location, but the adjacent counter has been converted specifically for hot meals. The former staff quarters have been demolished and replaced by a takeaway food station and a separate coffee bar. Along the western wall, a new salad bar has been installed, complemented by an outdoor barbecue area situated in the south-west. Furthermore, new doors have been opened along the southern perimeter, leading out to an expansive outdoor seating patio equipped with five sets of tables and chairs. Finally, the solitary trash bin has been upgraded to designated recycling bins, while the restrooms remain unaltered.
Cambridge Test 2 Task 2 • Official Candidate Script (4.png)
Awarded Band 7.0

Candidate's Submission:

People have differing views about the significance of the theatres and cinemas in the present age of technology. While some argue that there are no need for theatres and cinemas when it is possible to have access to online version of everything, in my opinion, that facilities are still of paramount importance for both people and economics. On the one hand, people today are able to watch any content they want through Internet without spending a lot of time and money Firstly, using electronic devise and social platforms makes it possible for users to watch any movies or films at any time they want. Shows and films are put at a certain time in cinemas, theatres which is not suitable for everyone. But if people use internet they can ensure the flexibility. Secondly, prices that have to be paid to enter cinemas and theatres are not affordable for many people However, through using the internet, to watch a movie or film can be much cheaper On the other hand, I, however argue that theatres and cinemas still have their significant part in terms of environment and financial benefits. For example, cinemas are provided with very big screens which can make people feel themselves inside the movie and in theatres watchers would enjoy the shows and and performances done by real actors, which is impossible to do in computers and mobile phone screens. On top of this, public theatres can bring the money that can be spent on public needs New roads and other facilities can be erected for public use with the financial funds taken from those cinemas, theatres. In conclusion, theatres and cinemas can be refered as non-useful places due to the developments in modern technologies but I believe that the use of them would bring a lot of advantages to both people and society

Official Examiner's Commentary:

Task Response: This is a strong response, with a series of ideas presented effectively. It starts with a note that different countries have different patterns and the writer's position is clear in the first paragraph [facilities are still of paramount importance]. The first half captures the benefits of watching performances and movies online; it saves time and money, people can watch at any time of day, with flexibility. Paragraph 4 presents the economic and cultural benefits, the big screen makes people feel more included in the experience, real live actors, who can do some things that computers cannot, public money can be raised from get togethers for specific needs. The candidate concludes that some people think that cinemas and theatres are [non-useful places] but they believe there could bring a lot of advantages.

Coherence and Cohesion: Ideas are logically organised, and cohesive devices are varied. Reference is used [when | those] but with some inaccuracy [that facilities]. Some effective linking devices are used [On the one hand | Firstly | Secondly | On the other hand | On top of this]. However, paragraph 2 is one sentence, and should be combined with paragraph 3 for a more logical progression.

Lexical Resource: Vocabulary is a strength, with frequent high-level items and some skilful collocation [of paramount importance | differing views | watch any content] although, some errors remain [devise / device] and there are some examples of less precise language [make people feel themselves inside the movie | watchers / audiences | non-useful places].

Grammatical Range and Accuracy: Grammatical structures are also varied and show some flexibility and some multi-clause sentences, including modal verbs and [if] clauses. However, most sentences are not error-free, as there are slips with articles, prepositions [in computers / on computers], other simple structures [is | are] and some missing punctuation.

Examiner's Recommendation for Band 8+:

To achieve a higher score, the candidate could make the paragraphing more consistent, and aim for a greater degree of accuracy in vocabulary and grammatical structures.

Model Exemplar • Task 2 Target Band 9.0

Examiner Band 9 Model Essay:

In the modern digital era, the ubiquitous availability of online streaming services and on-demand multimedia content has prompted some individuals to view physical theatres and cinemas as obsolete. However, others argue that these traditional entertainment venues remain indispensable institutions in both cultural and economic terms. In my view, while digital platforms offer unprecedented convenience and cost efficiency, brick-and-mortar theatres and cinemas continue to provide irreplaceable communal experiences and vital economic contributions that online alternatives cannot replicate. Advocates of digital entertainment primarily emphasize accessibility, personal flexibility, and financial economy. High-speed internet connectivity empowers consumers to access an almost limitless catalogue of films, series, and recorded performances anytime and anywhere, entirely liberated from the rigid timetables of conventional screenings. Furthermore, attending a commercial cinema or live theatrical production frequently entails substantial expenses on admission tickets, transportation, and concessions. For budget-conscious individuals and families, streaming media at home represents a significantly more economical alternative. Consequently, digital technology has undeniably democratized access to arts and entertainment. Nevertheless, physical cinemas and theatres fulfill critical cultural and economic functions that cannot be supplanted by home devices. Culturally, live theatre and cinematic auditoriums provide an immersive, collective atmosphere that fosters genuine social cohesion. The emotional resonance of witnessing a dramatic performance alongside an audience or experiencing cutting-edge audiovisual acoustics on a grand scale creates a profound psychological engagement that mobile screens fail to deliver. Economically, these venues generate substantial municipal revenue, create direct employment for actors, technical crews, and hospitality personnel, and stimulate adjacent commercial sectors such as restaurants and urban retail. In conclusion, although digital innovations have made entertainment consumption extraordinarily accessible and economical, physical theatres and cinemas remain fundamentally irreplaceable. They serve as essential cornerstones of communal culture and economic vitality, ensuring their enduring significance in contemporary society.
